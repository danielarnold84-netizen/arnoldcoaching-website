From understanding to speaking freely.

Six months of one-to-one German, built around the conversations you actually have — on the production floor, and with the people who come onto it.

Where we start

In our first conversation you understood my German introduction completely — and you noticed it yourself. The comprehension is already there. What stops you is the step into speaking.

That is a good starting position, and it decides the method. We are not building a certificate. Your employer asks for the improvement but sets no target level. So we work on the two situations that cost you information today: production colleagues who don't speak English, and external contractors on site — the ones where things arrive distorted or half-finished.

Everything below is built for that, and for a working week in Munich that is already full.

In your own words

“A conversation is running, and then it just goes black.”

A colleague reads it on your face and asks someone nearby to translate. Your definition of success was the opposite of that: carrying a conversation to the end, relaxed, with the words for it — and not sounding like the village idiot.

Three working fields

  • The production floor

    The colleagues you speak German with every day. From getting through a handover to asking the second question, and the third.

  • External contractors

    People who arrive, speak fast and leave. Saying precisely what you need, and checking that it landed — before the work starts.

  • Speaking before it is perfect

    The habit underneath both. Reacting, asking back, staying in the conversation instead of waiting for the right sentence.

How we work

  • →20 sessions of 60 minutesOne hour a week across six months — either one 60-minute session or two of 30. Online, so no travel time. Roughly 26 weeks, so holidays and shutdowns don't break the rhythm.
  • →Five minutes of German every dayNot homework. Five minutes daily does more than 90 minutes on a Saturday — this is the part that makes the weekly hour work.
  • →A goal session before we startFree of charge. We turn “better German” into two or three things we can actually measure in March.
  • →An open channel between sessionsSomething you need to say tomorrow, something that went wrong yesterday. Real situations, answered when they happen.
  • →Your own material, not a textbookVocabulary and phrases from your working context — the machines, the handovers, the contractors.
